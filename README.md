Audio
Smooth, relaxing background music by Peter Fitton — because your workday needs at least one thing that isn’t stressful.

Overview
Off & On Again drops you into the unpredictable world of IT support. Inspired by real tickets, fictional disasters, and the universal struggle of keeping tech alive, the game blends humour with relatable tech-support chaos.

Gameplay
Clock in at 8 AM, check your stock, and dive into a pile of generated repair jobs. Each task requires skill-based checks, and every action moves the clock toward 5 PM, when you finally escape home to unwind, destress, and try to stay sane enough to do it all again tomorrow.

Game built by a small team of freshly‑trained cyber security hopefuls and one indie dev who all collectively said, “Let’s make a full game in four weeks.”
And then… actually did it.
We’re still not sure if that was bravery, madness, or caffeine poisoning.

This wasn’t just coding — oh no.
We came up with the game idea, wrote the GDD, produced all the documentation, organised tasks, held daily Scrum stand‑ups, and ran a full 9:00–4:30 schedule every day like a studio that definitely wasn’t held together by instant coffee and stubbornness.
And when bugs got especially annoying, David spent a few weekends wrestling them into submission — because nothing says “indie dev” like unpaid overtime and shouting at a compiler.

If nothing else, we proved we can learn fast, build fast, and deliver something fun under pressure… even if our sleep schedules didn’t survive.

We’re a group aiming to break into the software or cyber security industry, and this project is our way of showing what we can do when given a challenge, a deadline, and a dangerously large jar of instant coffee.

If you’d like to support future projects (or help prevent us from replacing our bloodstream with espresso), feel free to buy us a coffee — or just follow our journey on social platforms.

If you’re an employer… well, you’ve already seen what we can do.
Imagine what we could build for you when we’re not racing a four‑week timer.

Remember:
Sometimes the fix really is turning it off and on again.
